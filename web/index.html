Controls

Hold Forward or Reverse to move: a short press creeps at about 4 km/h, keep holding and the car accelerates up to 20 km/h (10 km/h in reverse), the button fills up as the speed stage rises; release and the car brakes. Play

Each lesson teaches one manoeuvre with your car's own numbers: watch it, then drive it with the coach, and the help steps back as you pass: guided, then cue marks only, then practice, then a test where the stars count. ⟲ 5 s takes you back to try a bit again (that try is then practice).

      Driving model

      Kinematic single-track model about the rear axle, which is accurate at parking speed: the car pivots around a point on the rear-axle line, the inner rear wheel cuts inside the front wheels, and the front corner swings wide when reversing. The front wheels follow Ackermann angles. A short press of Forward creeps at 4 km/h; after one second of holding the target speed climbs by 4 km/h every second up to 20 km/h (reverse 3 to 10 km/h), acceleration is limited to 2 m/s² and releasing brakes at 3.5 m/s², and the steering self-centres as the car rolls (switchable). On the map the yellow lines show both leading corners; the thin cyan line is the end that swings: the inner rear wheel going forward, the outer front corner reversing.

      Parking sensors

      Twelve ultrasonic sensors as on the car: four in each bumper (two centre, two corner angled 35° out) and two per side. Each sees a 60° cone, 1.2 m ahead, 1.5 m behind, 0.6 m to the side, so a pillar beside a door is only heard once a corner sensor catches it, just like the real system. The beeper starts slow at the edge of range, speeds up through 1.0, 0.7 and 0.45 m, and becomes a continuous tone under 30 cm. Front sensors beep a higher note than the rear. Beeps need one tap on the wheel or a pedal first (phones only play sound after a touch). The sensor graphic at the right of the map works like the car's own screen: the bands around the car light up from the outside in, the end that is listening is brighter, a STOP card appears under 30 cm in the direction of travel, and the edge of the screen glows red on the side of the obstacle.

      What counts as parked

      All four corners inside the target bay lines, car stopped, heading within 6° of straight. The result is read once the car has stayed still for a second with no pedal held, so a last small nudge still counts. In your garage nose-in (as in the photos) or reversed both count; the result card shows your centring, angle and the gap to the bench. In a level the bay says which way round, and a space along a kerb counts when the car is between the cars with its tyres within 50 cm of the kerb.

      Course

      Ten lessons, from the first metres (a cone course) and how a car turns (a U-turn inside its painted swept path), through bays forwards, between cars, reversing in, parallel parking two ways, leaving a tight space and angled bays, to your own bay. Each one is a space and a route for your car, with the handbooks' rule of thumb next to the exact moment for this car: watch the ghost drive it, then drive it yourself. Guided, the coach shows each step and the next mark, waits until the wheel is set (hold it at full lock with one thumb while the other holds the pedal, or switch self-centring off in Setup), keeps you at walking pace and brakes on the mark; then only the marks are shown, then nothing (Show me and the coach on request), then a test where the stars count. Two passes take a level of help away, two misses bring it back in slow motion. After each try your path is drawn over the route and the coach names the first place it drifted more than 30 cm, and why. The routes use full lock or straight wheels only, as driving schools teach, so every step can be named exactly. Sources are the driving handbooks and schools listed on each lesson. The ⟲ 5 s button goes back five seconds so you can try a step again, anywhere except a lesson's test; a try with a rewind is practice, so it counts neither way and its stars are not saved.

      Learning layers

      Switch them in Setup. The path ahead shows where the car goes at the current steering and where it would touch first; the turning centre and circles show the point the car turns about, the circle the outer front corner sweeps and the circle the inner rear wheel runs on; the swept path draws all four corners' tracks; the ideal path draws the route that set par and where each move ends (in lessons the help level decides); the kerb close-up is a box at the bottom left, like a mirror tilted down, with the wheel nearest the kerb and its gap; the numbers give the car's angle to the space, the gap to the kerb and the gaps either side.

      Levels

      Three kinds so far: a row of bays entered nose first, the same reversing in, and parallel parking between two cars at a kerb on your right. Level 1 is roomy; by level 10 the bays are 2.3 m wide with a 5 m aisle, and the parallel space is the car's length plus 80 cm. Neighbours park off-centre, cars appear opposite, a pillar or a lamp post joins in. Kerbs only stop the tyres: the bumpers hang over them, as on a real street. Show me drives the planner's route; part way in, it plans from where you are.
