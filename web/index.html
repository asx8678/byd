Controls

Hold Forward or Reverse to move: a short press creeps at about 4 km/h, keep holding and the car accelerates up to 20 km/h (10 km/h in reverse), the button fills up as the speed stage rises; release and the car brakes. Drag the wheel round like a real steering wheel. The screen is a map of the bays, north up: it draws the path the car takes at the current steering, an outline of the car every 0.8 m along it and, in red, where it would touch something first; Whole area zooms out to the bays and the aisle, Follow car keeps the car in the middle. Keyboard: ← → steer, ↑ ↓ drive, Space straighten, X reset.

On the street, in Drive mode, the same two buttons are the accelerator and the brake: the higher your finger on the button, the harder it presses, and you can slide it while you hold. Let go of both and the car slows gently (regenerative or engine braking); it never rolls backwards. The map turns so you always drive up. Stop beside a free space on your right and Park mode takes over, with Forward and Reverse as above; hold Forward past 10 km/h, or tap Drive, to drive on. Keyboard: ↑ ↓ press the pedals gradually, P switches between Park and Drive.

Play

Each lesson teaches one manoeuvre with your car's own numbers: watch it, then drive it with the coach, and the help steps back as you pass: guided, then cue marks only, then practice, then a test where the stars count. ⟲ 5 s takes you back to try a bit again (that try is then practice).

      Garage layout

      The bays, pillars and walls come from the photo survey made in your other session, scaled from your Atto 2's rear track and wheelbase, the grey Clio's track and the drain cover in the aisle: about ±20 cm near 561, more further out.       Driving model

      Park mode (the garage, the levels, the lessons, and parking on the street) uses a kinematic single-track model about the rear axle, which is accurate at parking speed: the car pivots around a point on the rear-axle line, the inner rear wheel cuts inside the front wheels, and the front corner swings wide when reversing. The front wheels follow Ackermann angles. A short press of Forward creeps at 4 km/h; after one second of holding the target speed climbs by 4 km/h every second up to 20 km/h (reverse 3 to 10 km/h), acceleration is limited to 2 m/s² and releasing brakes at 3.5 m/s², and the steering self-centres as the car rolls (switchable). On the map the yellow lines show both leading corners; the thin cyan line is the end that swings: the inner rear wheel going forward, the outer front corner reversing. Each car's steering lock is worked out from its published turning circle, so the Smart, the Ram and the S-Class turn as their makers say. With rear-axle steering (the S-Class) the rear wheels turn against the front ones at parking speed, in proportion to them: the car then turns about a point ahead of the rear axle, the circle shrinks and the tail swings out a little further.

      On the street: Drive mode

      On the street the car runs on a second model, the way vehicle simulations commonly do: a single-track model whose tyres slip, with grip that runs out (front tyres a little softer in cornering than the rears, so a car turned too hard runs wide instead of spinning), weight that shifts forward under braking, ABS, the drive train's power through the driven wheels, slowing when you lift off, and air and rolling resistance. Each car is checked against what its maker publishes: the Atto 2 DM-i Boost reaches 100 km/h in 7.5 s, the Smart in 14.4 s and the S-Class 60 mph in 3.9 s, each tops out within 2% of its published top speed (the electronically limited ones exactly at it), and all stop from 100 km/h in 36 to 42 m. A tyre-slip model is wrong at walking pace, so below 7 km/h Drive mode moves the car exactly as the parking model does and between 7 and 18 km/h the two are blended, with no jolt as you pass through. The map shows at least the road you need to stop: your car, 1.5 s of travel and the distance to stop at 4 m/s² (14 m at walking pace, 49 m at 50 km/h); it zooms out quickly as you speed up, back in slowly, and not while you wait. Parking sensors switch off above 10 km/h, as real ones do. The figures that are estimated, not published (weight split, grip, drag), are listed with each car above.

      Harbour district

      A small district built by the map kit from a short description: four blocks inside a ring of streets, Harbour Street and Quay Road at 50 km/h, the narrower lanes at 30, parking lanes along most kerbs, a bus stop and a loading bay, and the harbour to the south. Traffic keeps right, and you park on your right, facing the way your lane runs. Nobody parks within 7 m of a junction. The parked cars come from the layout number (New layout gives another), and at least five free spaces fit your car with 1.4 to 2.6 m to spare (the tests have the route planner park every car in each of them, on several layouts). Gaps your car is long enough for are outlined in green; while you drive, the planner checks in the background that it can get your car into each, nearest first, and a gap that is too tight for it (a long car with a tail that swings out, say) drops off the map. Park mode starts when you stop beside one with your back bumper roughly level with the car in front of it; then it is the same parking as in the levels, with par worked out by the planner from where you stopped, and three stars. Traffic, lights and the rules of the road come next.

      Parking sensors

      Twelve ultrasonic sensors as on the car: four in each bumper (two centre, two corner angled 35° out) and two per side. Each sees a 60° cone, 1.2 m ahead, 1.5 m behind, 0.6 m to the side, so a pillar beside a door is only heard once a corner sensor catches it, just like the real system. The beeper starts slow at the edge of range, speeds up through 1.0, 0.7 and 0.45 m, and becomes a continuous tone under 30 cm. Front sensors beep a higher note than the rear. Beeps need one tap on the wheel or a pedal first (phones only play sound after a touch). The sensor graphic at the right of the map works like the car's own screen: the bands around the car light up from the outside in, the end that is listening is brighter, a STOP card appears under 30 cm in the direction of travel, and the edge of the screen glows red on the side of the obstacle.

      What counts as parked

      All four corners inside the target bay lines, car stopped, heading within 6° of straight. The result is read once the car has stayed still for a second with no pedal held, so a last small nudge still counts. In your garage nose-in (as in the photos) or reversed both count; the result card shows your centring, angle and the gap to the bench. In a level the bay says which way round, and a space along a kerb counts when the car is between the cars with its tyres within 50 cm of the kerb.

      Course

      Ten lessons, from the first metres (a cone course) and how a car turns (a U-turn inside its painted swept path), through bays forwards, between cars, reversing in, parallel parking two ways, leaving a tight space and angled bays, to your own bay. Each one is a space and a route for your car, with the handbooks' rule of thumb next to the exact moment for this car: watch the ghost drive it, then drive it yourself. The lessons are worked out for each car in Setup: in a Smart, a Ram or an S-Class the space, the route, the marks and the numbers are its own, it keeps the Atto 2's moves wherever it can drive them, the card says when it needs more, and a lesson it cannot do (a car too big for bay 561, the Ram in the tightest parallel space) is greyed out. Your progress is kept for each car. Guided, the coach shows each step and the next mark, waits until the wheel is set (hold it at full lock with one thumb while the other holds the pedal, or switch self-centring off in Setup), keeps you at walking pace and brakes on the mark; then only the marks are shown, then nothing (Show me and the coach on request), then a test where the stars count. Two passes take a level of help away, two misses bring it back in slow motion. After each try your path is drawn over the route and the coach names the first place it drifted more than 30 cm, and why. The routes use full lock or straight wheels only, as driving schools teach, so every step can be named exactly. Sources are the driving handbooks and schools listed on each lesson. The ⟲ 5 s button goes back five seconds so you can try a step again, anywhere except a lesson's test; a try with a rewind is practice, so it counts neither way and its stars are not saved.

      Learning layers

      Switch them in Setup. The path ahead shows where the car goes at the current steering and where it would touch first; the turning centre and circles show the point the car turns about, the circle the outer front corner sweeps and the circle the inner rear wheel runs on; the swept path draws all four corners' tracks; the ideal path draws the route that set par and where each move ends (in lessons the help level decides); the kerb close-up is a box at the bottom left, like a mirror tilted down, with the wheel nearest the kerb and its gap; the numbers give the car's angle to the space, the gap to the kerb and the gaps either side.

      Levels

      Three kinds so far: a row of bays entered nose first, the same reversing in, and parallel parking between two cars at a kerb on your right. Level 1 is roomy; by level 10 the bays are 2.3 m wide with a 5 m aisle, and the parallel space is the car's length plus 80 cm. The levels are built for the car chosen in Setup: a bay is wider and deeper by as much as the car is bigger than the Atto 2, while the aisles and the kerb space's room to spare stay the same, so a big car finds them harder; a layout it cannot get into at all is eased a step until it can. Neighbours park off-centre, cars appear opposite, a pillar or a lamp post joins in. Kerbs only stop the tyres: the bumpers hang over them, as on a real street. Show me drives the planner's route; part way in, it plans from where you are.
