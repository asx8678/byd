Review

What I'd change in the plan

The blueprint holds up. These changes make it easier to build and better to learn from.

Parking first, city later

Build the scenario generator and the tutorial before Drive mode and traffic. They teach what people come for, they are most of the free game, and they need none of the street physics. The city comes once parking is solid.

A route planner in the core

Par, "no impossible levels", Show me and the coach's cues all need one thing the blueprint assumed but didn't plan: a search that finds a way into the space for a given car. It is now its own milestone.

Fixed time steps, recorded inputs

The simulation steps 60 times a second whatever the screen does, and records your inputs. That gives exact replays, a ghost of your best attempt, fair scores, and tests that drive a whole lesson.

Every level re-checked on every build

The test suite solves every built-in level again, and generates and solves a few hundred seeds per template. If a change to a car or the physics breaks a level, the build fails.

Measured difficulty

After solving a level, the generator measures it: moves, the tightest gap along the route, spare room. The label comes from what it measured, not from the slider.

Coach text in data files

Lesson steps, tips and their sources live in files, not code. Wording can change without a release, and translations are a file each.

Accessible from day one

Sensor zones show shapes as well as colours. The controls can swap sides for left-handers, and lessons can run in slow motion.

Cars ship with a checklist

A car goes in only when its outline, wheelbase, overhangs and turning circle are verified. The W124's steering lock and the truck tractor block those two until measured. The name players see stays separate from the real model.

Your garage stays the anchor: bay 561 and its 128 replay checks remain the test every core change must pass.

Code layout

Where everything lives

The core stays pure TypeScript with no screen code, so it can still be ported to Swift. The content is JSON that both versions can read.

FileJob

test/Today's replay checks, planner cases, generator seeds, lesson replays, turning circles per car.

One collision rule for everything

The planner, the generator and the game use the same check: the body against everything, the mirrors only against things taller than 1.12 m, as today. Kerbs are new and low: the wheels may not climb them, but the bumpers may hang over them.

Scenario generator

Endless levels, none of them impossible

A level is a template, a difficulty and a seed. The same three always give the same level, so "Bays · level 6 · seed 4821" is the same space on every phone, and the daily level is just the date's seed. The search stops after a fixed number of steps, never after a number of seconds, so a slow phone and a fast laptop make the same level. Shipped and daily levels also carry their solved route and par, because tiny differences in maths between phone browsers could otherwise change a route.

How a level is made

  1. Pick a template and a seed.
  2. Turn the difficulty into settings: spare room, aisle or lane width, how neatly the neighbours parked, what is opposite, pillars.
  3. Build the scene: lines, kerbs, walls, parked cars and the target space.
  4. Solve it. The planner searches outward from the finished parking position until it reaches the entrance, then reverses that route. A car can drive any route backwards, so this finds the way in, a natural start and the number of moves in one search.
  5. Measure the moves, the tightest gap along the route, the spare room and the route length.
  6. Accept or skip. A seed with no route, or with a measured difficulty far from the one asked for, is skipped. "No route" and "the search gave up" are counted separately.
  7. Save the scene file with its par and the route, for Show me and the coach.

Rules that keep it fair

  • Every level has been solved before you see it.
  • You never start touching anything, and nothing that matters is hidden.
  • The time limit comes from the planner's route, with room to spare.
  • Par is the planner's best number of moves. That is not a proven minimum, so beating it is possible, and the game celebrates it.
  • Three stars, one per skill: no touching; accurate (centred within 15 cm in a bay, within 30 cm of the kerb, straight within 3°); efficient (par + 1 moves or fewer, inside the time).

Solving…

The coach's steps

    This level

    What the prototype showed: at the tight levels a forward entry needs about three moves where reversing in needs two, which is the handbooks' reason to reverse in, measured. The hardest kerb level (your car length + 0.8 m) solves in five or six moves. Bay 561 from the left comes out at three moves, the same as the earlier search.

    Tutorial

    A tutorial that teaches, then steps back

    The course is split into chapters, lessons and steps. A lesson is a scene (hand-made or generated), a car, a short script and the rules for its stars. The coach knows the route for the exact car you drive, so it can point at real moments instead of rules of thumb.

    StepWhat happensWhen

    WatchShow me: a ghost car drives the route and pauses at every cue.First time in a lesson, or on request

    TestNo help; the stars count.To finish the lesson

    The coach engine

    Each step is data: what the coach says, a condition that finishes the step, and checks that fail it.

    • Finish when: you reach a cue point (within 15 cm), the wheel is at a lock, the car has turned a set angle, you are stopped, a part of the car is level with a landmark, or you are parked in the target.
    • Fail when: you touch something, go faster than walking pace in a guided step, or drive the wrong way.

    Cues from the route

    The route for your car is cut into moves, and every change of steering or direction becomes a cue. The coach describes it with things you can see: your mirror against the other car's bumper, your rear wheel against a bay line, the car's angle. It shows the handbook's rule of thumb next to the exact moment, as the blueprint describes.

    Feedback that points at one thing

    After each try, your path is drawn over the planned one. The coach names the first place you drifted more than 30 cm from it, for example "you turned 0.6 m too early". The next try focuses on that step.

    Help that steps back

    There are four levels of help: the full coach, cue marks only, Show me on request, and none. Two clean passes lower the help, and two failures raise it again and turn on slow motion.
