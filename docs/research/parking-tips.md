# Parking techniques, rules and test standards

Collected on 4 October 2026 for the coach and lessons in the Parking Trainer Blueprint (`docs/blueprint.html`). Each point cites the numbered sources at the end. "Your cue" means a reference point the researcher was asked to check.

UK parks on the left, US/DE on the right, so tips should say "toward/away from the kerb". [n] is a URL in the list at the end; **Disagree** = sources conflict.

**1. Parallel parking**
- **Gap:** for beginners, about 1.5× car length [1][2]; the UK test uses about 2 [3]. Minimum: car length + 3 ft [4].
- **Line-up:** stop 2–3 ft (up to 1 m) beside the car ahead [5][6][7]. Rear bumpers level, or yours slightly ahead [4][1]. Start steering when your rear wheels are level with its rear bumper [6][7].
- **Lock (disagree):** US sources say full or sharp lock toward the kerb [5][6]. UK schools teach one turn of the wheel [7][8].
- **~45°** [4]: your centre door pillar [9] or front door [10] reaches its rear bumper, or your bonnet points to 2 o'clock [8]. Three-phase methods straighten the wheels here.
- **Counter-steer (your cue): confirmed by two state sources.**
  - Georgia, two-phase (lock in, then opposite lock): "stop when the side view mirror is in line with the rear bumper", then steer "rapidly in the opposite direction" [6].
  - Montana's detail sheet uses it after a straight phase: back "until your right side mirror is even with the bumper of the parked car" [9].
  - Montana's mirror is the kerb-side one. Georgia doesn't say which mirror, so the game must choose.
  - **Disagree:** most sources wait until your front clears the car ahead.
    - DVSA: "when you're sure that the front of your vehicle is clear of the parked vehicle" [1]. Pennsylvania [10] and Montana's activity sheet [9] agree.
    - New York: "front wheels are opposite the back bumper of the vehicle ahead" [5]. Oregon: right front tyre at its corner [2].
    - California: rear wheel within 18 in of the curb [4].
- **Kerb distance (disagree):** within 30 cm (1 ft) [5][7]. Georgia coaches 6–12 in [6]. California allows up to 18 in [4]. DVSA gives no figure [1].

**2. Bay parking**
- **Reverse-in (UK schools):** stop about 2 car lengths past the bay. Full lock when the bay's first line is level with the back seat in the rear passenger window [11] (variants: door handle [12], rear door [13]). Straighten when the lines look parallel in both mirrors [12].
- **Forward-in uses the mirror:** from 5–8 ft out, turn when your mirror reaches the target bay's own near line, not two bays before [6][9]. Your "two bays before" cue wasn't found in any fetched source.
- **Why reverse in:** "far less risky" [9]; you drive out forwards rather than reversing out half-blind [11][13]; usually easier [14]; Highway Code rule 201 recommends it for driveways [15]. **Disagree:** German multi-storeys often require forward parking [14].
- **Angled bays:** approach 3–4 ft out. Turn sharply as your steering wheel passes the first line, and stop 3–6 in from the kerb [6].

**3. Leaving a tight space**
- Back up to the car behind. Turn the wheels away from the kerb, signal and check both shoulders [5].
- Creep out while steering. Straighten when your front seat passes the car ahead's bumper [6].

**4. Low-speed principles**
- **Speed and look:** "Reverse slowly", check all mirrors and blind spots, and look "mainly through the rear window" [15]. Keep the clutch at the biting point [1]. "Walking pace" is driving-school wording only [16].
- **Dry steering (disagree):** DVSA says it wears tyres and steering [17]. Yet the test rates it "not worthy of recording" [18], and Bill Plant and Oregon both teach it [7][2].
- **Front swing:** "the front of your vehicle will swing out as you turn" [15]. When reversing, "the steered wheels are effectively at the back" [17]. No source found for a rear-axle pivot.
- **Long vehicles:** the rear wheels cut in toward the kerb [19][20]. A school bus can have up to 3 ft of tail swing [19].
- **Mirror:** tilt the kerb-side mirror down to see the kerb [14][21].
- **Get out and look:** if you can't see, get someone to guide you [15][22].
  - **Disagree:** DT1 is silent for cars. Johnsons says open the door to check [12]; RED says not on the test [16]. Lorry tests allow two exits [23].

**5. Signals and etiquette**
- **Signalling:** UK: before stopping or moving off [24], "if necessary" [15]. **Disagree:** DVSA: only if it helps someone [17]; California: even with no one around [25]. Germany: compulsory when pulling out [26], and per ADAC when turning into a space [27].
- **Claiming a space:** UK: "signalling does not give you priority" [24]. Germany (StVO §12(5)): first to reach the gap gets it, keeping priority while pulling past to reverse in or waiting for a car to leave [28]; ignoring it costs €10 [29]. No UK or US rule found.
- **Hazards, UK:** rule 116 says they "may be used when your vehicle is stationary, to warn that it is temporarily obstructing traffic", never "as an excuse for dangerous or illegal parking", and you "MUST NOT" use them "while driving" except for the motorway warning [24][30].
  - **Inference (no parking-specific source):** waiting stationary is arguably OK if the stop itself is legal. Hazards are not permitted while reversing in. Rely on your reversing lights [1].
- **Hazards, Germany:** only when you endanger others or are warning of danger. Misuse costs €5 [31][29]. ADAC treats hazards while double-parking as misuse [32].
- **Hazards, US:** varies by state [19]; California's §25251 doesn't list waiting for a space (other sections not checked) [33]. **Disagree:** North American truck manuals use flashers while backing [34][35].

**6. Trailer reversing**
- **Hand at the bottom:** the trailer goes the way your hand moves. Equivalently, turn the front wheels right (top of the wheel right) and the trailer goes left [36][37].
- **Follow and correct:** once the trailer turns, steer back to follow it. Correct drift by moving the top of the wheel toward the drift [38]. This is the same rule with the opposite framing, so don't mix the two unexplained.
- **Small inputs:** sharp corrections cause a jackknife [36], where the towing vehicle stops following the trailer's track and the rig folds [39].
- **Pull forward:** pull forward to straighten and retry [38][40]. Past 90°, stop and drive forward [37].

**7. Truck dock backing**
- **Sight side:** back toward the driver's side, "even if it means going around the block" [41]. The blind side is the passenger side [34]. These sides are mirrored in right-hand-drive countries (inference).
- **G.O.A.L.:** OSHA lists "Get Out And Look", a spotter and backing slowly [42]. FMCSA requires GOAL in driver training [43].
- **Setup:** pull past the alley, parallel to its outer boundary [44], then angle the trailer toward the target [45]. No official source found for the swing-away step.
- **Spotters:** agree hand signals including "stop" [41]; stop if you lose sight of the spotter [46]. OSHA (construction): a blocked rear view needs an alarm or observer [47]. **Disagree:** UK HSE uses banksmen only as a last resort [48].
- **Alignment:** older CDL alley dock: within 3 ft of the rear, straight, at most 2 looks [44]; 2022 test: 2 free pull-ups and 2 exits [49]; Maine: within 12 in [50]. Real docks: centred and square [51]; no numeric tolerance found.

**8. Smart parked nose-to-kerb**
- **Germany: unclear.** §12 covers where to park and saving space, not orientation [28]. Marked bays must be followed [52], and parking across bays is an offence [53]. A 2005 local court allowed it (non-binding, secondhand) [54].
- **France: unclear.** R417-1 only sets which side of the road [55].
- **Italy: illegal unless signed.** Cars must park "parallelamente" (parallel to the kerb), fine €42–173 [56]. An early-September 2026 draft would allow it for cars up to 2.70 m; it was not in force as of then [57].
- **UK:** no orientation rule [58]. Bay markings decide (London code 24) [59]. A ticket in Stroud was overturned [60].
- **US: illegal unless angle parking is authorised.** Cars must be parallel to the curb, within 18 in (CA) [61] or 12 in (NY) [62]; Richmond police: "not legal" [63].

**9. Test standards**
- **UK (DT1):** parallel "reasonably close to and parallel with the kerb… within about two car lengths" [64]. Bay: "Parking outside the bay is unacceptable", but position within it is judged leniently [3]. Faults: brushing the kerb, too far out, acute angle, straddling bays [65]. No cm figure; the popular "30 cm" isn't official.
- **Georgia:** "not more than 18 inches from the curb", in a 22 × 10 ft box [66].
- **New York:** within 1 ft [5].
- **Pennsylvania:** 24 × 8 ft box. No touching uprights, lines or kerb; "one attempt… no more than three adjustments" [67].
- **Texas:** a wheel over the curb or hitting a standard rejects the test [68].
